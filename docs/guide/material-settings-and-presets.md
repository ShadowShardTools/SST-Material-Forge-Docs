---
title: Material settings and presets
sidebar_position: 5
description: Inputs a material offers, and named presets of their values.
---

Inputs are the settings a material offers: a wear amount, a tint, a switch. They belong to the material rather than to any one node, so the same value can drive several nodes at once, and the Viewer can change it without opening the graph.

1. In Forge, open the **Inputs** panel down the left of the graph. Press **+** and choose a type: Boolean and Float stand at the top, the rest in submenus by kind — **Vectors** (Vector 2–4), **Matrix** (2x2, 3x3 and 4x4), **Color & Texture**, **Shape** and **Text** — that open beside it, or with Right Arrow. The menu floats over the window, so a small graph never cuts it off. A Float, a vector or a matrix has an **Int** checkbox that keeps it to whole numbers, and a Float in Int mode is an integer, on an integer port. Both offer **Spline**, a curve (see *2D Splines* below). A material also offers **Enum** (named choices — type them comma-separated under Options, and the graph reads the index chosen), **Color** (a picker and an alpha), **Texture** (one of the project's imported textures), **Text** (a string, on as many lines as you like), **SVG** (one of its imported drawings), **Font** (an imported font or a generic family) and **Gradient** (a ramp of colors, edited as a Gradient Map's). A subgraph's inputs are ports, where the first three would only be a Float in Int mode or a Vector 4 by another name; its **Text**, **SVG**, **Font** and **Gradient** inputs take what the subgraph's node is wired to — a material's input, set in the Viewer, or a Gradient Constant — and their own default where nothing is, so a decal or a palette made once as a subgraph is set by every material that uses it.
2. Select the input to name it and set its **default** under Properties. A Float or vector input also has a **Mode**: **Regular** types its numbers, and **Slider** drags them over a range you set with **Min** and **Max** — which is also how they are set in the Viewer, so a slider says what values an input is meant to take. Choosing Slider widens its starting range to keep the value already set.
3. Drag it onto the graph to read it. Drag it as many times as you like — every **Input** node reads the same input, so changing it changes them all. The **Text** and **SVG** nodes take inputs into their settings rather than as images: click the plug beside any of their settings — the words, the font, the drawing, the size, the offsets, the color — to show that setting's port, and wire an input to it. A **Text**, **Font** or **SVG** input sets the words, the face or the drawing; a number, a boolean, an enum or a color sets the setting of that kind, kept within the setting's own range. The setting then takes what the input is set to, and Properties shows *Set by the wired input* in its place. **2D Spline Text Outline** takes the same, and **2D Spline Text** its Text and Font. The **Gradient Map** does the same with its gradient: expose it, wire a **Gradient** input to it, and whoever uses the material recolors it in the Viewer — rust to fresh paint, moss to dry — without opening the graph. A **Constant** set to **Gradient** can be wired there too: one ramp, the same for every map wired to it, for a palette several parts of a material share.
4. Arrange them by dragging one row onto the edge of another, or with <kbd>Alt</kbd> and the arrow keys. That order is the order the settings are listed in, and the order of the ports on a subgraph's node. Right-click a row to **Duplicate** or **Remove** it, or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>D</kbd> on it: the copy goes under it, with its type, default, range, heading and Viewer setting, named next in its series — **Wear** gives **Wear 2** — and read by no node until you drag it onto the graph.

A **Material** input takes a whole material — at the foot of the **+** menu, for a material or a subgraph. Choose its **Profile** in Properties — a built-in one, or **Custom**, then drop a profile of yours on the field that appears under it, or press the field's round button to pick one, as on an Output (until one is chosen, Custom takes what Simple PBR would): it takes only materials of that profile, or of a profile under it, and its **Input** node gives what the profile has — a port for each of the profile's channels (an OpenPBR one shows a layer's channels with that layer switched on in Properties) and each of its outputs — read from the material chosen, at each one's fallback where that material leaves it unfilled. Its **Out** is the material itself, to wire into a subgraph's or a material node's own Material input. Its default, in Properties, is a field like a Custom profile's: drop a material from the Explorer on it, or press its round button for a window of the materials that fit — the project's under **Project**, and your asset library's under **Asset Library** (one chosen from there is added to the project first, with its package). One that does not fit is refused, with the reason. A floor can take its tiles, and a wall its bricks, as materials someone picks.

Subgraphs have profiles too — an interface: **New SubGraph Profile** in the Profiles folder makes one, under the built-in **SubGraph** or another subgraph profile, and in Properties it declares **Inputs** as well as **Outputs**: "Pattern" takes a Scale and gives a Mask. Choose **Custom** as a subgraph's **Profile**, on its Output node, and drop the profile on its field, and the subgraph has those inputs and outputs, among its own — named and typed by the profile, and kept while it has them. A **SubGraph** input, at the foot of the **+** menu beside Material, asks for a subgraph profile — **SubGraph**, or **Custom** with one of yours on its field: it takes only subgraphs of that profile, or of one under it, and its **Input** node *runs* the subgraph chosen — the profile's inputs are ports on it, so what you wire in is what the subgraph runs on (an input left unwired, and any text, font, drawing, material or subgraph input, is the subgraph's own default), and the profile's outputs come out of it. Its **Out** is the subgraph itself, to hand to another graph's SubGraph input. Its field, in Properties and in the Viewer, is a Material input's: drop a subgraph on it, or pick one of those that fit, the project's or the asset library's. A material can then offer its pattern, its wear or its noise as a choice in the Viewer.

Defaults are Forge's business and settings are the Viewer's, and the two halves are independent. Forge shows the material as it is built — every input at its default — whatever anyone has set in the Viewer; the Viewer shows it as it is set. Editing in Forge changes the default and nothing else: a preset the Viewer is showing neither moves nor goes out of date because the graph was edited, and choosing **Default** there is what brings the Viewer back to the values Forge holds. Exports match the half you start them from.

In the **Viewer**, the material's inputs appear under **Settings**, below the presets — a number for each component, a checkbox for a boolean, a picker for a color, a box for text, a list of imported images, drawings or fonts for a texture, an SVG or a font, a field for a Material (or SubGraph) input, as in Properties — drop a material (or subgraph) from the Explorer on it, or press its round button to pick one of those that fit, from the project or the asset library; one that does not fit is refused, with the reason — and the 3D View follows as you change them. An input has no range of its own, so its value is any number, positive or negative. A setting sits *over* the default rather than replacing it: the default stays what Forge says it is, and is what the material goes back to.

**Presets**, above the settings, are named sets of those settings, chosen from a dropdown:

- **Default** is always in the list. It is not stored with the others: it is the material with no settings at all, so choosing it puts every input back to its default.
- **Save current settings as a preset** stores what the material shows now under a name, which you can type straight away.
- A star after the name — **Worn\*** — means the settings have changed since that preset was chosen. Updating the preset keeps them and takes the star away; choosing another preset replaces them.
- The three buttons beside the dropdown act on the chosen preset: the tick **updates** it with the current settings, the pencil **renames** it, and the bin **deletes** it. None of them apply to Default.
- Presets and the current settings are saved with the project, so one material can carry several looks — rusted, painted, wet — without copying it. A preset changes settings only; the nodes are left as they are built.
