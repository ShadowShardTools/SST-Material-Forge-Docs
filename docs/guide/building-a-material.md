---
title: Building a material
sidebar_position: 3
description: Adding and connecting nodes, adjusting settings, and the Output node that ends every material.
---

1. **Add nodes.** Right-click empty space in the graph (or press <kbd>Space</kbd>) to open the node menu. Type to search by name or by what you need (for example “voronoi”, “albedo” or “glitter”), or open a category and pick a node. The node is placed where you clicked. To browse instead, open **Window → Nodes**: every node of the menu, by category, as tiles or a list — drag one onto the graph to place it there, or double-click it to add it in the middle of the view.
2. **Connect them.** Drag from an output port on the right of a node to an input port on the left of another. Let go over empty canvas instead and the node menu opens there: pick something and it arrives already wired to the port you pulled from — pulling from an input asks for something to feed it, and from an output for something to feed. Drag from a connected input to move or remove its wire, or <kbd>Alt</kbd>-click a wire to disconnect it.
3. **Adjust settings.** Select a node to see its settings under Properties. Drag a slider, type an exact value, or focus a slider and use the arrow keys.

   A color opens a picker from its swatch: a saturation square, a hue slider, and hex, RGB or HSL fields — the small **RGB** / **HSL** button switches which. Where a color has an opacity, as the Constant node and each gradient stop do, the picker carries an alpha slider for it — that is the only place it is set, beside the color it applies to rather than further down the panel. The **pipette** beside the field picks a color from the screen, where the browser offers it.
4. **Send results to the material.** Every material ends in one **Output** node, which lists the channels it has — Base Color, Normal, Metallic, Roughness, Height, Ambient Occlusion and Emission, and more for OpenPBR — each with the number of values it takes. Connect your results to those slots; whatever reaches them appears in the 3D View and is exported, and empty slots keep the material's defaults. The node is part of the material, so it cannot be deleted or copied.

   Two settings on the node say what the material is:

   - **Profile** — what the material gives: the channels of its shading model, and any outputs its profile adds. Three are built in, the shading models themselves — **Simple PBR**, **Flat Color** and **OpenPBR**. **Simple PBR** is a lit surface with the channels above. Its **Emission** is light the surface gives off whatever lights it — screens, lava, glowing runes — added to what it reflects, in color like Base Color; black, or nothing connected, gives off none. **Emission Luminance** multiplies it — 1, or nothing connected, as it is; 0 puts it out; above 1 brighter than white. **Flat Color** is a plain unlit shader: its Base Color alone, drawn as it is, with no lighting, no reflections and no relief.
   - **OpenPBR** is the OpenPBR Surface standard: a surface built in layers, the way real materials are — fuzz over a clear coat over a base that is metal or dielectric, the dielectric's gloss over diffuse, subsurface scattering and transmission. It keeps Simple PBR's channels, under the names they have there — Base Color is OpenPBR's base color, Metallic its base metalness, Roughness its specular roughness, Normal its geometry normal, Emission its emission color, Opacity its geometry opacity — so a material switched between the two keeps its wires. Beside them are OpenPBR's own: **Base Weight**, **Diffuse Roughness** (from smooth Lambert at 0 to a dusty, flattened look at 1), **Specular Weight** and **Specular Color** (how strong the gloss is, and its tint), **Anisotropy** with a **Tangent** map for its direction (brushed metal) and **Anisotropy Rotation** to turn that direction about the surface (0 to 1 is a whole turn), **Specular IOR**, and **Emission Luminance**, which multiplies Emission as in Simple PBR. Each optional layer is a switch in Properties that adds its channels:
   - **Profiles of your own** live in the **Profiles** folder of a package: right-click it and choose **New Material Profile** (or **New SubGraph Profile**, below). Select one to set it up in Properties: its **Parent** — a built-in profile or another of yours — and the **Outputs** it adds, each a name and a type (Float or Vector 2–4). A profile has every output of the ones above it: "Terrain" under Simple PBR with a **Wetness** output, "Wet Terrain" under it with **Puddles**, has Simple PBR's channels, Wetness and Puddles. Choose **Custom** as a material's **Profile**, then drag the profile from the Explorer onto the field that appears under it — or press the field's round button, which opens a window of every profile of that kind: the project's under **Project**, and those kept in your asset library under **Asset Library**, as tiles to search, with what the one selected is under and adds below; double-click one, or select it and press **Use** (one from the library is added to the project first, with its package; **None** leaves the field empty) — and its outputs appear on the Output among the material's own, named and typed by the profile and kept while it has them; an output added to the profile later appears on every material of it, and one taken away stays on them as their own. A material's profile goes with it into an exported package, and so do the ones above it.
     - **Coat** — a clear or tinted varnish over everything: car paint, lacquered wood. Coat Color tints what lies beneath it, Coat Darkening is how much the light caught under it deepens the base's color, and it has a roughness, anisotropy (with its own rotation), IOR, normal and tangent of its own.
     - **Fuzz** — a soft sheen of fibres over the top: velvet, dust, peach skin.
     - **Thin Film** — an iridescent film a fraction of a micrometre thick (Thickness in µm): soap bubbles, oil on water, heat-tinted metal.
     - **Transmission** — light through the body: glass, water, gems. **Transmission Color** is the color the light becomes at **Transmission Depth** (in centimetres; 0 tints the surface instead), **Transmission Scatter** clouds it (smoky quartz, murky water), and **Dispersion** with its **Abbe Number** splits white light into colors, as diamond and crystal do.
     - **Subsurface** — light that wanders under the surface before coming out again: skin, wax, marble, milk. **Subsurface Color** is how the surface looks overall — exactly: it is the light that comes back out of the material, matched to how much its own surface reflects back in — **Subsurface Radius** (centimetres) how far light travels inside, and **Radius Scale** how much further each of red, green and blue goes — red furthest, as in skin.

     **Thin Walled** makes the surface a sheet with no inside — a leaf, paper, a bubble — that light passes through rather than into a volume. Lengths in OpenPBR are in centimetres; the 3D View's models count as 20 cm across, something held in the hand. The Ray Traced view shades every layer in full. The Raster view shows them in real time, as close as a real-time renderer gets: the coat (at its own IOR), fuzz, thin film (under the coat too, its colours in what passes through), anisotropy and transmission are all there, glass refracting the environment as the Ray Traced view does, and subsurface as the colour it gives the surface from afar; Diffuse Roughness, a metal’s Specular Color edge, light scattering inside glass and light wandering under the skin need Ray Traced. Exported, each channel is a map of its own, and the engine presets read the shared channels.
   - **Surface Type** — **Opaque** is solid; **Transparent** adds an **Opacity** channel and blends the material where it is thin.
   - **Alpha Clipping**, in Properties when the Output node is selected — a switch and a **Clip Threshold**. Either surface can clip: the material is drawn where its **Opacity** reaches the threshold and cut away where it does not, which is how leaves, grates and fabric edges are made. Turning it on gives the material an Opacity channel even when it is Opaque.

   The settings decide which channels the material has, so choosing Flat Color drops the channels it has no use for, and disconnects what fed them. That happens in the same step as the change, so one <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> brings both back.

   **Add output** under the slots gives the material an output of its own: a slot to wire, named and typed under **Custom Outputs** in Properties — Float, Vector 2, Vector 3 or Vector 4, which is what its port is colored and counted as. Nothing shades it; it is baked and saved beside the material's channels, named after it (`wall_curvature-mask_1024.png`), for whatever reads it. Declare as many as the material needs.
