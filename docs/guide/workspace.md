## Forge and the Scene Viewer

The editor has two tabs, beside its name in the menu bar:

- **Forge** builds the material out of nodes, and uses it: click a material in the Explorer and Properties shows its presets and settings, with its bakes — change a setting and the 2D and 3D Views follow it as you do (see [Material settings and presets](./material-settings-and-presets.md)). **File → Bake maps…** and **Bake all presets…** bake the material open.
- **Scene Viewer** uses a scene, without the nodes: what you set, save as presets and bake is the material on the scene's model. Pick a scene in the Explorer and its material is the one shown on the right. Picking a material changes nothing here; drop one on the model to put it on the scene's model. An imported model lists its parts — its material slots — in **Materials**, above the presets, each with the material it wears, or **Default** where none has been put on it yet (it is drawn in the plain default look). Pick a row and the presets, settings and bake on the right are that material's; the first part is picked until you pick another. Drop a material on a row to put it on that part, and press the cross beside one to take its material off. A built-in shape has no parts, and is one row, **Model**.

The Scene Viewer has the same Explorer Forge has, scenes and packages and all, so anything can be made, found and renamed there too. An editor last left in the Material Viewer, which there was before, opens in Forge.

## The panels

| Area | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| Menu bar | The File, Edit, Arrange, View, Window and Help menus on the left, the Forge and Scene Viewer tabs and the working resolution and precision in the middle, and undo and redo on the right |
| Explorer | Packages, folders, materials, subgraphs and imported textures. Every package has **Materials**, **Subgraphs**, **Profiles** (with **Material Profiles** and **Subgraph Profiles** inside), **Custom Nodes** and **Resources** (with **Textures**, **Environments**, **Models**, **Fonts**, **Vector Images** and **Color LUTs** inside), each colored like what it holds; they are part of the package and cannot be renamed, moved or deleted. Right-click a row to make something where it is — **Create material** under Materials, **Create subgraph** under Subgraphs, **Import textures…** under Textures (or any import under Resources, each filed in its own folder), and **Create folder** in any of them — or to rename or delete it; the header itself holds only **New package** and **Delete**. Folders of your own go inside those folders, never beside them — and not loose in Profiles or Resources, which only hold their own — and stay in the one they were made in. A package saved with older folders opens with them renamed, and with what they held filed in the folder for each kind. Drag a row onto the edge of another to move it beside it, or into the middle of a folder to file it there; <kbd>Alt</kbd> with the arrow keys does the same from the keyboard. Creating, renaming, moving and deleting here all undo with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>, along with the edits in the open graph |
| 3D View | The material on a sphere, cube, plane, cylinder or knot — or on a model of your own, imported into Resources — lit like a real surface, seen through the scene's camera in use (see *Cameras* below) and framed to its picture's shape. The camera's **Renderer**, set in its Properties (pick the camera in the Explorer), draws it **Raster**, at once and as you turn it, or **Ray Traced**: light is followed as it bounces, so the model shadows itself, reflects itself and picks up light bounced between its folds. A ray traced picture starts grainy and clears over a few seconds while nothing moves, its samples counted at the foot of the view, up to the camera's **Preview Samples**, set in its Properties — 1024 to begin with, anything from 1 to 65536, kept with the camera. Render Image gathers the camera's **Render Samples** instead, set apart from them in its Properties, so the view can settle quickly while a render takes as many as the finished picture is worth. Raise it and a settled picture gathers on from where it stopped; changing anything shown starts it again. While you turn the view it is drawn raster, so it follows the pointer smoothly, and the ray traced picture takes over as soon as it stops (*Ray traced when still*). With the camera's **Denoise** ticked, the picture is cleaned by Open Image Denoise as it settles — quickly at 16 samples, again at 64 and 256, and most carefully at 1024 and at the last sample — so a clean picture comes long before the samples alone would give one; the count says *denoised* while it is shown. Untick it to see the samples as they are. It is lit by one of the scene's environments, which the camera chooses, and shows that environment behind the model or a color of the camera's (see *Environments* below); the **Render Profile** it is shown through is the camera's too (see *Render Profiles* below). An environment's **Map** field takes a map dragged from the Explorer, or press the field's round button for a window of them as thumbnails: those imported into the project under **Project**, and the asset library's under **Asset Library** — the built-in maps, and any EXR or HDR of yours, which is added to the project as an environment map once chosen. Maps are the environment maps of the asset library's Built-in section, the image files in the project's `resources/library/HDRIs` folder |
| 2D View | The selected node's output, optionally tiled, one channel at a time or all together — or, pinned, one node's output whatever is selected. Save As writes it at any resolution |
| Graph | The nodes of the material or subgraph you are editing. Each graph panel shows a graph of its own |
| Inputs | The open material's or subgraph's inputs, down the left of the graph. The double arrow hides it, and the handle it leaves behind brings it back |
| Properties | The selected node's settings — or, pinned, one node's |
| Status bar | Node count, resolution, GPU memory in use and evaluation time |

Panel dividers can be dragged, or focused and moved with the arrow keys; <kbd>Esc</kbd> during a drag puts one back.
